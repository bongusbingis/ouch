# Visual review and release evidence

This policy applies to UI, HUD, icons, 3D art, animation and VFX, including small refinements. A functioning control or a passing contrast check can still be visually wrong. An advisor verdict is evidence for one bounded review; it is never owner acceptance, upload authorization or production readiness.

## Compare before deciding

Before editing, name the changed component's family and inspect at least two comparable existing elements when available. Record where those peers appear, what they share and any legitimate role difference. For UI, compare foreground/background pairing, font/weight/stroke, label baseline, content padding, visible corner radius, border/shadow, icon treatment and interactive states. For 3D/VFX, compare silhouette, palette/material response, scale, detail density and motion/readability in the same environment. Inspect actual visible artwork bounds, not just its image or mesh container.

Use the canonical Figma system and accepted Studio presentation as references. A reward card's body text is not automatically a precedent for an action button. Accessibility, readability and aesthetic consistency must be satisfied together; a local improvement does not justify an unexplained family exception. Prefer the existing component or token. When a family needs a new treatment, compare the whole family and obtain the already-required direction approval before broad propagation.

An accepted surrounding composition limits the scope of edits, not the scope of inspection. Always use surrounding controls as comparators. Avoid leading review questions, lists of supposed improvements, or instructions to ignore unchanged peers. Give the reviewer the user's requirement, before/after evidence and named comparators; ask it to identify contradictions independently. Visual packets bind source hashes but omit implementation code/rationale from the image critique.

## Evidence and decisions

Use `tools/reviewer.py gemini --visual-packet <packet.json> --task <requirement>` at meaningful milestones. Small changes still receive Codex's peer comparison and visual inspection; they join the next milestone packet rather than require a paid call per edit. A connectivity/exploratory call requires `--smoke-test` and cannot satisfy readiness. Plain-text historical APPROVED responses cannot satisfy the new check.

The packet contains `schema_version: 1`, `purpose: milestone|release`, `scope`, `requirement`, `coverage_limits`, selected `sources`, their `capture_sources` SHA-256 map, `required_cases`, `cases`, and `families`. Verify those source hashes against the actual Studio source in a fresh play session when capturing; do not relabel old captures with current repository hashes. Each case has `id`, `device`, `state`, `before` and `after` image paths. Each family has `id`, named `targets`, named `peers`, explicit `target_names` and `peer_names` lists, their `relationship`, and a `reference` such as `desktop:after`. Images must show the changed elements in their full surrounding composition at native capture resolution. Close-ups can supplement a case, not replace its context. Include the smallest supported viewport and representative desktop, relevant safe-area/reflow boundaries, long content and affected hover/focus/pressed/selected/disabled/loading states. Record omitted cases as limits, not implied passes. A release packet must cover the complete agreed visual scope and unresolved issue ledger, not just the latest change.

The wrapper requires observations, comparisons and cited evidence for every case in seven categories (including a separate comparison for every named family): family, hierarchy, typography, geometry, color, assets and states. Every named peer must be explicitly compared. Peers may appear in the same full-screen capture as the target; they must be different controls. Identical generic observations across categories are rejected. An observable defect is a finding even when small. Missing evidence is unverified. Either prevents an advisory pass. Fix and recapture defects; disputed findings need an evidence-backed disposition in the task record and a new review that addresses the dispute. Do not silently downgrade a defect to optional, erase a finding, edit an advisor response or invent owner approval.

Inspect actual Studio output yourself before and after advisor feedback. Verify the change in context at normal size, then zoom into details. Where practical, compare measured peer properties as well as images. Verify interaction states through actual input; label fixtures and emulator evidence truthfully. Neither structural checks nor AI image review can prove aesthetic quality or complete device behavior.

## Readiness and freshness

`tools/visual_review.py --review <review-directory>` checks a structured advisory pass, complete case/category coverage, no unresolved findings, original result/contract hashes and unchanged evidence, policy and runtime files. Files added, edited or removed after review invalidate it. The record is durable under `.review`; interrupted or failed reviews never become passing records. This is an ordinary workflow safeguard, not tamper-proof security against someone rewriting the repository.

The local `lune run src/scripts/lune/release-readiness.luau` additionally requires a full release-purpose review and the owner's existing final visual sign-off. Configure their paths in `tools/workflow.json`. Only after the owner explicitly accepts the exact reviewed candidate may their acceptance be recorded as JSON with `decision: ACCEPT`, their actual `statement`, and the result's `review_sha256` plus `contract_sha256`. Agents must never manufacture that statement or treat elapsed time, a direction approval, or an advisor pass as acceptance. No acceptance is requested for unfinished milestone work.

The standalone asset gate remains responsible for frozen upload approval and moderation, not in-game composition quality. Refocus every upload approval request as already required. Existing place-publishing CI remains unchanged under the owner's earlier instruction; it does not enforce this local visual preflight. The owner must run the preflight before pushing a release. A hard block on every direct production push would require adding this check to the publishing path and its repository protections; do not claim that exists.

## Why the September 8 action review failed

The cyan action labels were changed to dark ink based on isolated readability. Their neighboring cyan navigation labels used white. The reviewer received a rationale that favored the new treatment and no mandatory peer-by-peer audit. Technical tests checked bounds and behavior; the upload gate was not involved because no content was uploaded. The formatted APPROVED response was overstated in the handoff. The owner rejected the inconsistency. This is a failure of comparison and interpretation, not a new universal rule that colored surfaces must use white text.
